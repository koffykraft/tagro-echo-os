# TAGRO × ECHO OS — Planar / Prismatic Evolution Rule V1

Status: active core structural contract
Authority: Constitution v1.1, DEC-0022
Effective: 2026-08-22

## 1. Purpose
This contract prevents TAGRO ECHO from becoming trapped by first-stage choices, sunk cost, tool familiarity or local optimisation.

When an existing tool, environment, provider, architecture, design or workflow is persistently flawed, repeatedly creates friction/risk, is materially inadequate for TAGRO's future, or when a credible materially better option appears, the alternative must be considered through due diligence.

Consideration is mandatory when the trigger is credible. Replacement is not automatic.

## 2. Evolution trigger
A Planar/Prismatic review is triggered when one or more of the following are evidenced:
- recurring defects that survive reasonable remediation;
- repeated workarounds or duplicate systems;
- persistent user friction, confusion or avoidance;
- inability to meet mobile/offline/field requirements;
- security, privacy, resilience or recovery weakness;
- unacceptable cost or operational burden;
- scaling limits inconsistent with TAGRO's foreseeable future;
- lock-in that makes replacement or migration unreasonably difficult;
- technology/environment becoming obsolete or unsupported;
- materially better capability becoming available;
- an architecture/design forces business reality into the tool rather than the tool serving reality;
- repeated evidence that a different environment or interaction model would materially improve correctness, comfort, speed, continuity or replaceability.

## 3. Planar review
The Planar review examines the candidate across all materially affected dimensions rather than one subsystem.

At minimum consider:
- Identity;
- Event;
- Evidence;
- Relationship;
- Time;
- Location;
- Authority;
- State;
- Provenance;
- Confidence.

Then examine affected operational planes/receivers, including where relevant:
- Sell / Billing;
- Service;
- Stock;
- Purchase;
- Cash / Payments / Bank;
- Logistics / Delivery / Returns;
- Documents;
- Accounting / BUSY;
- Warehouse / analytics;
- Observer / attention;
- staff, mechanic, manager and owner experiences;
- mobile, tablet, desktop, offline and external-provider environments.

A solution that improves one plane while damaging another must disclose that trade-off.

## 4. Prismatic review
The Prismatic review prevents false convergence on a narrowly framed solution.

If evidence is split, the problem is poorly framed, or the current implementation biases the comparison, step outward and consider broader solution classes.

Examples:
- do not compare only two JavaScript libraries when the real question may be PWA vs native vs hybrid;
- do not compare only two logistics providers when the real question may be a multi-carrier orchestration layer;
- do not redesign a page repeatedly when the real problem is workflow/context architecture;
- do not patch a database choice indefinitely when the real problem is the persistence/event model;
- do not keep an AI feature because AI exists if deterministic interaction is faster and safer.

The current implementation is evidence, not the boundary of the prism.

## 5. Due-diligence record
A replacement/admission recommendation must document:
1. problem/evidence;
2. current solution and persistent limitations;
3. candidate alternatives, including at least one materially different class when relevant;
4. Planar consequences;
5. Prismatic expansion and why convergence is justified;
6. security/privacy;
7. performance/reliability;
8. cost and operational ownership;
9. UX/accessibility/field use;
10. mobile/offline consequences;
11. integration/downstream consequences;
12. migration/coexistence;
13. rollback/recovery;
14. data/evidence/provenance preservation;
15. vendor lock-in / replaceability;
16. future TAGRO scale/operating-model suitability;
17. test/pilot evidence required before admission.

## 6. Sunk-cost prohibition
Existing investment is a migration cost to be evaluated, not a reason to suppress a better solution.

An earlier choice may remain because it is still the best total solution after review. It may not remain merely because replacing it is emotionally, organisationally or technically inconvenient.

## 7. No churn rule
This contract does not authorize fashionable rewrites or continuous technology churn.

A proposed replacement must show material advantage or risk reduction relative to migration cost and disruption.

Novelty is not evidence.

## 8. Migration truth
Replacement must preserve admitted facts and evidence or explicitly migrate their meaning.

No migration may silently turn:
- unknown into known;
- queued into completed;
- inferred into confirmed;
- one actor into another;
- one business event into a different event;
- stale evidence into current evidence.

## 9. Coexistence
Where safer, old and new implementations may coexist temporarily behind explicit boundaries for comparison, shadowing, readback or phased migration.

Coexistence must not create two competing sources of truth.

## 10. Final test
The preferred solution is the one that best serves TAGRO's real future operating system across the whole field — not the one that best preserves the current implementation.
