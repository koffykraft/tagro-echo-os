# TAGRO × ECHO OS — Comfort & Appeal Design Rules V1

Status: active companion design contract
Scope: all user-facing ECHO operational surfaces
Authority: subordinate to the ECHO Constitution, Product Design Engineering Contract, Page Ecology Contract and ECHO UI/UX Design Rules V1

## 1. Purpose

ECHO shall be operationally correct and also comfortable enough that people prefer using it over paper, memory, ad-hoc messaging or older software.

Comfort and appeal are not decorative extras. They influence hesitation, fatigue, error rate, willingness to continue, confidence and adoption. They therefore belong to product engineering.

The objective is not visual novelty. The objective is a calm, capable, recognisable operating environment that reduces mental and physical effort while preserving business truth.

## 2. Desired emotional state

ECHO should make the user feel:

- oriented — I know where I am;
- calm — the screen is not shouting at me;
- confident — ECHO heard what I did;
- in control — I can undo, edit, correct or recover appropriately;
- relieved — I do not have to remember what the system already knows;
- fast — routine work moves without friction;
- trusted — local, accepted, approved and downstream states are never confused.

ECHO shall avoid creating anxiety through excessive warnings, unexplained states, visual clutter, tiny controls, hidden actions, unpredictable rearrangement or irreversible-looking interactions.

## 3. Visual character

The ECHO visual character is:

CALM · CLEAN · WARM · OPERATIONAL · PRECISE

It is not:

- futuristic-for-its-own-sake;
- dense ERP chrome;
- colourful dashboard decoration;
- glass/effects-heavy consumer fashion;
- sterile grey administration software;
- playful where business consequence demands seriousness.

Use light surfaces, restrained contrast, generous whitespace where it improves grouping, thin structural borders, readable typography and limited accent colour.

TAGRO orange may identify primary action, active state or meaningful attention. It shall not cover large portions of the interface merely for branding.

## 4. Comfort hierarchy

Every screen shall visually answer in this order:

1. What am I doing?
2. What do I need to touch or enter now?
3. What important context must remain visible?
4. What happened after my action?
5. What secondary detail is available if I need it?

Visual weight must follow this hierarchy.

## 5. Spatial comfort

- Primary work receives the largest uninterrupted area.
- Controls must have breathing room sufficient for confident touch.
- Density comes from removing irrelevance, not shrinking necessary controls.
- Repeated actions should remain spatially predictable.
- Destructive or opposing actions must not sit so close that hurried use risks mis-touch.
- Important identity/context may remain compactly visible as external memory.
- Phone layouts should favour vertical flow and thumb reach.
- Desktop may reveal useful context/evidence alongside work rather than simply stretching phone layouts.

## 6. Typography

- Editable phone text should normally remain at least 16px-equivalent to avoid focus zoom and strain.
- Primary task/value receives stronger size/weight than explanatory text.
- Labels use ordinary operational language.
- Uppercase may be used sparingly for short, high-recognition actions such as SELL, ACCEPT or REVIEW; it should not dominate long text.
- Secondary technical/provenance detail is visually quieter but remains readable.
- Do not use tiny grey text to hide information that is actually important.

## 7. Colour

Colour communicates priority and state, not decoration.

- Neutral/light surfaces form the normal working environment.
- TAGRO orange is a restrained action/attention accent.
- Success, warning, error and review states must remain distinguishable without colour alone.
- Avoid simultaneous use of many saturated colours.
- Avoid assigning permanent decorative colours to every module.
- A user should be able to understand the page hierarchy in greyscale.

## 8. Shape, depth and layering

- Rounded corners may soften controls/cards, but radius must remain restrained and consistent.
- Borders and spacing establish structure before shadows.
- Depth is reserved for actual layers: drawer, review overlay, temporary sheet, dialog or elevated consequential step.
- Do not use floating cards everywhere.
- Do not use translucency unless it clarifies layering and preserves contrast/performance.

Principle: depth communicates state, not fashion.

## 9. Icons

- Icons improve recognition but do not replace words for important operational actions.
- Prefer familiar, simple, high-contrast shapes.
- Icon style must be consistent across ECHO.
- Decorative illustrations do not compete with the current job.
- Model/product imagery may be useful where visual identification materially speeds selection or reduces error.

## 10. Motion and micro-feedback

Motion is functional punctuation.

Allowed purposes:
- acknowledge a tap;
- show state transition;
- reveal/hide contextual layers;
- preserve spatial continuity;
- direct attention to a meaningful change.

Avoid:
- decorative looping animation;
- long transitions;
- confetti/reward effects for routine business actions;
- motion that delays the next job.

Typical feedback should be immediate and quiet:

- Saved locally
- Waiting to send
- ECHO accepted
- Needs approval
- Recorded
- Corrected

## 11. Familiarity and muscle memory

- Primary actions keep stable locations within a workflow.
- Personalisation may change suggestions or prominence, not randomly rearrange core controls.
- Repeated forms should use predictable focus/Enter behaviour.
- Familiar real-world working geometry may be retained where it reduces learning cost, provided it does not preserve a known usability defect.

## 12. Reduce typing fatigue

Comfort includes clerical reduction.

ECHO should default, remember, lookup, suggest, calculate, scan or infer safely where policy permits.

The user should not repeatedly type:
- enterprise;
- authenticated identity;
- ordinary branch context;
- today's date;
- known tax rate;
- product IDs that can be found by search/scan;
- repeated customer/machine facts already established.

AI suggestions remain assistive evidence/proposals and may not silently establish consequential truth.

## 13. Interruption comfort

A comfortable operational app does not punish interruption.

- Draft state persists continuously where practical.
- Returning to a workflow restores meaningful context, not merely the module landing page.
- The user can tell what was saved and what remains unsent.
- Switching briefly to another ECHO job should not destroy the first job.
- Browser/app resume should preserve the current customer's or machine's identity where safe.

## 14. Error comfort

Errors must be specific, local and recoverable.

Prefer:
"Select a product before recording the count."

over:
"Validation error."

- Preserve entered work after failure.
- Put correction near the cause.
- Do not make normal users read stack/runtime terminology.
- Consequential failure must state what did NOT happen.

Example:
"Saved on this phone. ECHO has not accepted the bill yet."

## 15. Empty and first-use states

Empty states should orient and invite work, not look broken.

Examples:
- No open service jobs. ACCEPT MACHINE
- No pending sync.
- No stock counts in this session. FIND ITEM

First-use help should appear contextually, be brief, and become unobtrusive after familiarity develops.

## 16. Appeal without distraction

Appeal comes primarily from:
- good proportion;
- visual rhythm;
- strong alignment;
- comfortable spacing;
- high-quality typography;
- predictable interaction;
- thoughtful empty states;
- restrained accent colour;
- immediate feedback;
- polished transitions;
- appropriate imagery where recognition benefits.

Appeal does not require visual novelty.

## 17. Role character

The same visual system may express different working moods without becoming different products.

Counter: energetic, quick, minimal.
Service intake: calm, reassuring, machine/customer identity prominent.
Mechanic: low-stress, task-focused, durable/clear.
Manager: concise, exception/approval-oriented.
Owner: quiet situational awareness, material attention before metrics.

## 18. Real-world comfort testing

A screen is not comfortable because it looks good in a browser screenshot.

Test:
- standing at a counter;
- one-handed use;
- phone keyboard open;
- repeated numeric entry;
- ordinary mid-range Android hardware;
- bright ambient light;
- variable network;
- interruption/resume;
- hurried use;
- first-time and familiar users.

Observe grip changes, hesitation, repeated taps, scrolling, paper fallback and questions asked aloud.

## 19. Comfort & Appeal Gate

Before admitting a surface, ask:

1. Does it look calm before the user reads it?
2. Is the next action visually obvious?
3. Can the common job be performed without visual hunting?
4. Are controls physically comfortable to touch?
5. Has unnecessary typing been removed?
6. Does the page preserve memory/context for the user?
7. Is feedback immediate but quiet?
8. Are errors recoverable without losing work?
9. Does the screen remain attractive after repeated daily use, not only at first glance?
10. Would a real staff member voluntarily choose this over the previous method?

## 20. Final principle

ECHO shall hide technical complexity without hiding business truth.

The product should feel intelligent because the user has less to remember, type, search and repair — not because the interface advertises intelligence.

The desired experience is:

LESS HUNTING · LESS TYPING · LESS FEAR · LESS CLUTTER
MORE ORIENTATION · MORE CONTROL · MORE SPEED · MORE TRUST
